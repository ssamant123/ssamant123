# Powering the Machines: CleanSpark, Riot, and the Race to Become AI's Landlords

**A narrative investment report on the power-infrastructure opportunity**
**Prepared:** June 25, 2026

*Analytical synthesis of public information for discussion. Not investment advice;
verify against primary filings before acting. Site-level figures cited from company
8-Ks, earnings transcripts, and reporting as of mid-2026.*

---

## The thesis in one sentence

The most valuable thing a Bitcoin miner owns in 2026 is no longer its Bitcoin —
it is the electricity it is allowed to consume, and the two companies examined here
are quietly sitting on a combined **~3.5 gigawatts of contracted, grid-connected,
shovel-ready power** at the precise moment the AI industry has discovered that
power, not chips, is the binding constraint on its growth.

---

## Why this is happening: the bottleneck moved

For a decade, the knock on Bitcoin miners was that they did something economically
frivolous with enormous amounts of electricity. That criticism aged into the thesis.
The thing that made miners look reckless — building out gigawatts of cheap,
interconnected power years ahead of any obvious need — turned out to be the single
hardest asset to acquire in the AI buildout.

Training and serving frontier AI models is fundamentally an energy problem.
Hyperscalers can buy GPUs with a purchase order; they cannot buy a grid
interconnection with one. New large-load connections to the U.S. grid now face
**multi-year queues**, and the best transmission-adjacent sites are gone. As one
sell-side framing (Bernstein) put it, miners' secured power makes them *critical
partners* for AI providers staring down interconnection delays and grid congestion.

The numbers describe a stampede. Public miners have collectively announced **more
than $70 billion in cumulative AI/HPC contracts**; IREN alone signed a **$9.7B deal
with Microsoft** for 76,000 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs and controls roughly 3 GW. The
revenue-mix shift is the tell: listed miners may derive **up to 70% of revenue from
AI/HPC by the end of 2026, up from ~30% at the start of the year.** This is not a
side hustle anymore; it is the main act, and the market is repricing these companies
from commodity miners (cyclical, BTC-correlated, low multiple) toward digital-
infrastructure landlords (contracted, recurring, infra multiple).

The catch worth underlining: AI workloads need **firm, consistent power**, not the
interruptible load that made miners useful for grid balancing. The winners are the
operators whose sites can deliver always-on, high-density compute — which is exactly
what separates a press release from a signed lease.

## Riot Platforms: the pivot that already pays

Riot is the more advanced story because it has stopped talking about optionality and
started booking it. The clearest signal came in early 2025, when management did
something a pure miner would never do: it **halted a 600 MW Phase II Bitcoin-mining
expansion** at its Corsicana, Texas campus and hired consultants (Altman Solon) to
evaluate AI/HPC uses for that capacity instead. Mining was no longer automatically
the highest and best use of a megawatt.

The crown jewel is **Corsicana — up to 1.0 GW of ERCOT-approved power**, of which
only ~400 MW is used for mining today. Riot is building out the rest as data center:
plans call for **up to 756 MW of IT capacity across ~4.5 buildings (168 MW each)**,
with the first phase — internally "Project Ditto," ~335,000 sq ft, ~$400M, running
April 2026 through 2028 — already permitted and under construction. This is the
difference between *having* power and *delivering* compute: core-and-shell going
vertical, not a slide deck.

Proof of revenue arrived through the **AMD lease at Riot's Rockdale site** — an
initial 25 MW of critical IT load delivered in phases through May 2026, an option
already exercised to **50 MW**, and a path to **up to 200 MW**. That anchor tenant
turned into Riot's **first meaningful data-center income of $33.2M in Q1 2026.** A
real hyperscaler-class customer, a real contract, real dollars in the P&L.

Then there is the moonshot: a **memorandum of understanding with Terrestrial Energy**
to co-locate nuclear (390 MW IMSR modular reactors) with data centers, contemplating
a scale-up toward **~4 GW.** Treat this as a free option on the decade, not a base
case — but it signals where management's ambition points: from "miner with spare
power" to "vertically integrated AI power developer."

Put together, Riot's **~1.7 GW of approved capacity** is being valued by bulls at
**$9–21B of potential equity value** if monetized at data-center comparables —
against a current market cap near $10.8B. The bet is that the market is still partly
pricing Corsicana as mining megawatts rather than leased IT capacity.

## CleanSpark: the overlooked powerhouse, one signature away

If Riot is the pivot that pays, CleanSpark is the larger, quieter land bank that the
market hasn't fully woken up to. CleanSpark holds **~1.8 GW of contracted capacity**,
with **over 1 GW already live across 33 sites** concentrated in Georgia, Tennessee,
Mississippi, and Wyoming. Crucially, its operating model is **vertically integrated by
design**: CleanSpark *buys the land, builds the substations, and operates everything
itself.* That means it owns sites wired directly into high-voltage substations —
infrastructure that can feed new compute clusters **without** waiting years for new
grid interconnection. In an environment where the substation *is* the moat, that is
the whole game.

The portfolio is deliberately being positioned for conversion:
- **Sandersville, Georgia (250 MW)** near Atlanta — the marquee site, and the subject
  of **live talks with Meta** to lease capacity for AI/HPC. A signed hyperscaler lease
  here is the single largest catalyst for the stock.
- **Texas / Greater Houston** — a deliberate new-market entry: **Sealy (285 MW
  approved, ~200 MW energizing in 2027)** with substation construction underway, and
  **Brazoria (600 MW in two phases, first 300 MW ERCOT-approved).**
- **Jackson, Tennessee (60 MW)** and **Cheyenne, Wyoming (110 MW)** — flagged for
  prospective AI/HPC conversion, alongside Washington.

Management's framing is the cleanest articulation of the whole sector thesis:
**"Mining funds the platform; AI monetizes it."** The Bitcoin operation throws off
the cash and justifies the power contracts; the AI lease layer is where the
infrastructure-multiple value gets unlocked. CleanSpark has also demonstrated the
*flexibility* side of the asset — curtailing hundreds of megawatts of mining load in
Tennessee on demand — proving its sites behave like dispatchable grid assets, a
property utilities and hyperscalers both value.

The honest gap: CleanSpark's AI revenue is still **prospective.** Riot can point to
AMD dollars in the income statement; CleanSpark, for now, can point to Meta
*conversations* and a superior raw land/power bank. That is precisely the source of
both the discount and the upside — the market is pricing the optionality, not the
contract. The day a Sandersville lease is signed is the day the narrative converts to
numbers.

## How to read the two together

| Dimension | Riot Platforms (RIOT) | CleanSpark (CLSK) |
|---|---|---|
| Power story | ~1.7 GW approved; Corsicana 1 GW flagship | ~1.8 GW contracted across 33 sites |
| AI monetization | **Proven** — AMD lease, $33.2M Q1 DC income | **Prospective** — Meta talks, no signed lease |
| Build status | Core-and-shell under construction (Project Ditto) | Substations being built; sites convertible |
| Differentiator | Largest single-site campus + nuclear option | Vertically integrated land + substation control |
| Re-rating trigger | Lease-up of Corsicana IT capacity | A signed hyperscaler lease (Meta/Sandersville) |
| What you're paying for | Execution that's already underway | Optionality not yet contracted |

The simplest way to hold the thesis: **Riot is the de-risked core** — you are buying
demonstrated conversion of power into contracted AI revenue, with Corsicana lease-up
as the next leg. **CleanSpark is the higher-torque option** — a larger, integrated
power bank trading at a discount because its AI revenue is still a signature away. A
single Meta lease re-rates the latter; continued Corsicana lease-up compounds the
former.

---

## What would prove the thesis — and what would break it

**Proves it:** a signed CleanSpark hyperscaler lease; Riot leasing the remaining
~600+ MW of Corsicana IT capacity; AI/HPC climbing toward the ~70% revenue-mix the
sector is forecast to hit; firm-power deals (incl. Riot's nuclear MOU) advancing.

**Breaks it:** AI demand cooling or compute-per-watt improving faster than expected,
softening the power-scarcity premium; sites proving harder/costlier to upgrade from
interruptible mining load to firm AI-grade power than modeled; financing strain (both
names still carry meaningful capex and, for CLSK, ~$1.8B of convertible debt) if
Bitcoin — currently ~25% below estimated production cost — stays depressed and starves
the "mining funds the platform" engine before AI revenue scales.

The elegant part of the thesis is that the downside in mining and the upside in AI
share the same asset: the megawatt. When BTC is weak, the value migrates to leasing
it; when BTC is strong, mining funds more of it. These companies are, increasingly,
not Bitcoin bets with a power side-business — they are **power-infrastructure bets
with a Bitcoin cash engine.**
