## The one-paragraph pitch

CleanSpark and Riot are priced by the market as Bitcoin miners. They are
increasingly **power-infrastructure companies with a Bitcoin cash engine** — together
controlling **~3.5 GW of contracted, grid-connected, shovel-ready electricity**, the
single scarcest input in the AI buildout. As hyperscalers hit multi-year grid
interconnection queues, that secured power is being monetized into long-term AI/HPC
leases priced *independently of Bitcoin*. We recommend **overweighting RIOT as the
de-risked core** (AI conversion already booked) and holding **a smaller, speculative
CLSK position** (larger power bank, catalyst pending), sized for the reality that both
remain high-beta proxies on a Bitcoin price currently ~25% below production cost.

## Why now: the bottleneck moved from chips to power

Training and serving frontier AI is fundamentally an energy problem. A hyperscaler
can buy GPUs with a purchase order; it cannot buy a grid interconnection with one —
new large-load connections face **multi-year queues**. Bitcoin miners spent a decade
accumulating exactly that scarce asset. The repricing is underway and measurable:

- Public miners have announced **>$70B in cumulative AI/HPC contracts** (IREN alone:
  $9.7B with Microsoft, ~3 GW controlled).
- Listed-miner revenue mix is forecast to shift from **~30% AI/HPC to ~70% by
  year-end 2026.**
- The market is repricing the group from a *commodity-miner* multiple (cyclical,
  BTC-correlated) toward a *digital-infrastructure* multiple (contracted, recurring).
- **Key nuance:** AI needs *firm, always-on* power, not the interruptible load that
  made miners useful for grid balancing. Winners are operators who can deliver
  AI-grade critical IT capacity — separating signed leases from press releases.

## The two companies

### Riot Platforms (RIOT) — the pivot that already pays
- **Halted a 600 MW Bitcoin-mining expansion** to repurpose capacity for AI/HPC — a
  pure miner would never do this.
- **Corsicana, TX:** up to **1.0 GW ERCOT-approved**; only ~400 MW used for mining.
  Building up to **756 MW of IT capacity** (4.5 buildings); first phase "Project
  Ditto" (~$400M) under construction, 2026–2028.
- **AMD lease (Rockdale):** 50 MW contracted, expandable to 200 MW → **first
  meaningful data-center income of $33.2M in Q1 2026.**
- **Terrestrial Energy MOU:** nuclear (390 MW IMSR) co-location, scalable toward ~4 GW
  — a free long-dated option.
- Balance sheet: **18,005 BTC (~$1.6B)**, ~$310M cash, lower leverage than CLSK.
- Bull-case monetization of ~1.7 GW → **$9–21B potential equity value** vs. ~$10.8B
  cap.

### CleanSpark (CLSK) — the overlooked, integrated power bank
- **~1.8 GW contracted**, >1 GW live across **33 sites** (GA, TN, MS, WY).
- **Vertically integrated:** buys land, builds its own substations, operates it all —
  can energize compute *without* waiting in the interconnection queue. The substation
  is the moat.
- Marquee catalyst: **Sandersville, GA (250 MW)** — **live Meta talks** for AI/HPC.
- Texas entry: **Sealy (285 MW approved)**, **Brazoria (600 MW, first 300 MW
  ERCOT-approved)**; plus Jackson TN (60 MW) and Cheyenne WY (110 MW).
- Management framing: **"Mining funds the platform; AI monetizes it."**
- Most efficient self-mining fleet (~50 EH/s), but carries **~$1.8B convertible debt**
  vs. ~$1.0B equity; AI revenue still **prospective (zero signed)**.

## The numbers: BTC-price sensitivity (annual mining gross profit)

Gross profit = (BTC price − cash cost/BTC) × annualized production. Excludes D&A,
SG&A, interest, and treasury marks. Illustrative; production held constant.

| BTC price | **RIOT** (6,060 BTC · $44,629 cost) | **CLSK** (7,900 BTC · ~$40,000 est. cost) |
|---|---|---|
| $50,000 | ~$33M | ~$79M |

| $100,000 | ~$336M | ~$474M |

*CLSK cost/BTC is an estimate (not disclosed) off a ramping fleet — indicative only;

RIOT's.* The AI lease layer (BTC-decoupled) sits on top of all of this.

## Three ways to be right (and the risks each must respect)

| Voice | Core claim | RIOT | CLSK | Must avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | Power plants priced as miners; re-rating underway | Buy — core | Buy — torque | Scarcity premium fading |
| **Bear** | Leveraged proxy on a below-cost commodity, sold as a story | Underweight | Avoid | Missing a real re-rating |
| **Skeptic** | Right asset, wrong certainty — trade proof, not promise | Buy pullbacks | Small option, add post-signature | Letting the commodity engine size the bet |

**Our posture is the skeptic's:** own the scarce asset, pay for proof, size for
survival, let signatures decide when to add. It is the only stance that doesn't
require resolving the unknowables in advance.

## Key risks & monitorables
- **Bitcoin < $60K** — the tripwire; pressures liquidity, the convert math (CLSK), and
  the "mining funds the platform" engine. Trim, don't average down.
- **AI/HPC execution** — deal signings and lease pricing; CLSK's Sandersville
  signature is the single biggest CLSK catalyst.
- **Demand-side erosion** — faster compute-per-watt gains or hyperscaler self-builds
  (incl. their own reactors) compressing the power-scarcity premium.
- **Conversion cost/time** — upgrading interruptible mining load to firm, AI-grade
  critical power is expensive and slow.
- **Crowding (RIOT)** — after a 200%+ run, part of the re-rating is already priced.

---

## The elegant core of the thesis
The downside in mining and the upside in AI **share the same asset: the megawatt.**
When Bitcoin is weak, value migrates to leasing power; when Bitcoin is strong, mining
funds building more of it. These are, increasingly, **power-infrastructure bets with a
Bitcoin cash engine — not Bitcoin bets with a power side-business.**
