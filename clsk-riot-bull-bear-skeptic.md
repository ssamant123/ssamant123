# Three Ways to Be Right About CleanSpark and Riot

**A narrative investment report in three voices: the Bull, the Bear, and the Skeptical Risk-Taker**
**Subjects:** CleanSpark (NASDAQ: CLSK) · Riot Platforms (NASDAQ: RIOT)
**Prepared:** June 25, 2026

*Analytical synthesis of public information for discussion. Not investment advice.
Verify against primary filings before acting. Figures may be delayed or restated.*

---

## Setting the table

Two U.S.-listed Bitcoin miners are trying to become AI's landlords. Between them
they hold **~3.5 GW of contracted, grid-connected power** — the scarcest input in
the AI buildout — at a moment when Bitcoin trades **~25% below its estimated
production cost** (~$64K spot vs. ~$84K to mine). Riot (~$10.8B cap) has already
turned power into contracted AI revenue (AMD lease, $33.2M Q1 data-center income).
CleanSpark (~$4.5B cap) holds the larger, vertically integrated land-and-substation
bank but its AI revenue is still prospective (Meta talks, no signed lease).

The same facts support three coherent, intelligent conclusions. Here they are, each
argued in good faith.

---

## I. The Bull — "You are buying power plants at a miner's multiple"

The bull starts with a single observation: the market is still pricing these
companies as Bitcoin miners, and that is a category error. The binding constraint
in artificial intelligence is no longer GPUs — it is electricity, and the right to
draw it from a grid that now takes years to interconnect. Riot and CleanSpark spent
a decade acquiring exactly that right. What looked like a wasteful habit is now the
hardest asset in technology to replicate.

The proof is no longer theoretical. Across the sector, public miners have announced
**over $70 billion in AI/HPC contracts**; IREN signed **$9.7B with Microsoft.** The
revenue mix is forecast to swing from ~30% AI/HPC to **~70% by year-end 2026.** This
is a structural re-rating from a commodity multiple to an infrastructure multiple,
and it is happening in real time.

**For Riot**, the bull doesn't have to imagine the future — Riot halted a 600 MW
mining expansion to build data center instead, has Corsicana (1 GW approved) going
vertical as up to 756 MW of IT capacity, signed AMD (50 MW, expandable to 200), and
booked $33.2M of data-center income in a single quarter. Bull-case monetization of
its ~1.7 GW at data-center comps implies **$9–21B of equity value** against a
~$10.8B cap. You are paying for megawatts and getting the AI lease-up as a call
option that is already being exercised.

**For CleanSpark**, the bull sees the bigger, cheaper prize. ~1.8 GW across 33 sites,
vertically integrated — it owns the land and builds its own substations, so it can
energize compute without waiting in the interconnection queue. The Meta conversation
at Sandersville (250 MW) is the lit fuse; a single signed hyperscaler lease re-rates
the entire equity. Management's framing — *"mining funds the platform; AI monetizes
it"* — is the whole thesis: the BTC engine pays for the power bank while the AI layer
unlocks infrastructure value.

The elegant part: **the megawatt is the asset in every weather.** Weak Bitcoin pushes
value toward leasing power; strong Bitcoin funds building more of it. Heads the
infrastructure wins, tails the mining pays. **Own RIOT as the de-risked core; own
CLSK as the higher-torque option on the next signature.**

---

## II. The Bear — "A leveraged bet on a commodity below cost, wrapped in a story"

The bear is unimpressed by the narrative and points at the income statement. Strip
away the slides and you have two companies whose **core business is mining a
commodity that currently sells for a quarter less than it costs to produce.** That is
not a footnote — it is the entire cash engine. CleanSpark just reported a **$378M
quarterly loss**; difficulty fell 10% as rigs went dark; hashprice sits near
breakeven for anyone without elite efficiency. "Non-cash mark-to-market" is the
phrase used to wave this away, but the marks are real money the equity has lost, and
they reveal how violently the value swings on one input nobody controls.

Now layer on the balance sheet. CleanSpark carries **~$1.8B of convertible debt**
against ~$1.0B of equity. If Bitcoin stays depressed, the "mining funds the platform"
engine sputters precisely when the company needs cash to build the AI infrastructure
that justifies the multiple — forcing dilution, asset sales, or refinancing into a
weak tape. The bull's tidy "heads I win, tails I win" ignores that **building data
centers costs billions of dollars that have to come from somewhere**, and right now
they come from a loss-making commodity operation and capital markets.

The AI pivot itself is shakier than the deck suggests. AI needs **firm, always-on,
high-density power** — not the interruptible mining load these sites were built for.
Converting an interruptible megawatt into AI-grade critical IT capacity is expensive
and slow, and the bull's "$9–21B if monetized" leans on the word *if*. CleanSpark's
AI revenue today is **zero signed dollars** — it is conversations. Even Riot, the
"proven" one, books a whole $33.2M quarter of data-center income against a $10.8B
market cap; the valuation is discounting leases that don't exist yet.

And the demand side is not guaranteed. AI compute-per-watt keeps improving;
hyperscalers are building their own power and reactors; the power-scarcity premium
that makes these sites precious could compress faster than the conversions complete.
**RIOT has run 200%+ — much of the dream is already in the price.** The bear's verdict:
this is a high-beta, capital-hungry, debt-laden proxy on a below-cost commodity, sold
as an infrastructure stock. Underweight, or avoid.

---

## III. The Skeptical Risk-Taker — "Right asset, wrong price discipline; trade the proof, not the promise"

The third voice agrees with the bull about *what these companies are* and with the
bear about *what could go wrong* — and resolves the tension with position sizing and
trigger discipline rather than conviction.

The skeptic concedes the core insight: **the megawatt is a genuinely scarce, genuinely
valuable asset, and these two own a lot of it.** That is not hype; the interconnection
queues and the $70B of sector contracts are real. So the asset is right. But the
skeptic refuses to pay infrastructure prices for mining-grade certainty. The
discipline is simple — **pay for what is contracted, treat what is merely talked
about as a free option, and never let the commodity engine size the position.**

That cashes out to an asymmetric, two-sleeve approach:

- **Riot is ownable today**, because the skeptic is buying *demonstrated conversion*
  — AMD dollars in the P&L, Corsicana under construction — not a promise. But after a
  200%+ run, the skeptic buys *pullbacks toward the low-$20s*, not breakouts, and
  sizes it as a core-but-bounded position. The next-leg catalyst (leasing the
  remaining ~600+ MW of Corsicana IT capacity) is concrete and trackable.

- **CleanSpark is a deliberately small, high-torque option.** The skeptic *wants* the
  exposure to a signed Meta/Sandersville lease — that single event re-rates the equity
  — but refuses to underwrite it. So the position is sized to survive being wrong:
  small enough that the ~$1.8B debt and the below-cost Bitcoin tape can't force a
  capitulation, large enough that a signed lease matters. The skeptic adds *on
  weakness*, never chases, and pre-commits to scaling up only **after** a contract is
  signed — paying up for certainty rather than betting on it.

The skeptic's risk controls are explicit. **Bitcoin below ~$60K is the tripwire** —
that is when the cash engine and the convert math turn dangerous, and the skeptic
trims rather than averages down into a balance-sheet problem. The skeptic also watches
the *demand* side the bull ignores: any sign that compute-per-watt gains or
hyperscaler self-builds are eroding the power-scarcity premium is a thesis-level
warning, not noise.

The synthesis: **this is the right asset to own and the wrong asset to be confident
about.** So you own it in the proportion the evidence supports — more of the one with
signed revenue, less of the one with a pending signature — and you let the catalysts,
not the narrative, decide when to add. You are not betting that the story is true; you
are betting that *if* it becomes true you'll be paid asymmetrically, and that *if* it
doesn't you'll have sized small enough to be wrong cheaply.

## The three verdicts at a glance

| | Core claim | RIOT stance | CLSK stance | Fatal flaw it must avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | Power plants priced as miners; re-rating underway | Buy — de-risked core | Buy — torque option | Demand/scarcity premium fading |
| **Bear** | Leveraged proxy on a below-cost commodity | Underweight — priced for a dream | Avoid — debt + zero signed AI revenue | Missing a genuine re-rating |
| **Skeptic** | Right asset, wrong certainty; trade proof not promise | Buy pullbacks, bounded size | Small option, add only post-signature | Letting the commodity engine size the bet |

**The honest bottom line:** all three are internally consistent because the outcome
genuinely hinges on two unknowables — where Bitcoin trades over the next 12–18 months,
and how fast contracted AI revenue replaces speculative AI optionality. The skeptic's
posture is the one that doesn't require you to resolve those unknowns in advance: own
the scarce asset, pay for proof, size for survival, and let the signatures do the
talking.
