## 2. Thematic synthesis

### Theme 1 — Pathologic and occult contralateral nodal rates in bilateral neck dissection series (TORS / HPV+)

**Rates vary with case mix. Studies enriched for base of tongue (BOT) and midline tumors report about 20% occult disease. Cohorts of cT1–2, cN0–1 lateralized tumors report 0–4%.**

| Series | Population | Contralateral pN+ | Occult (cN0 contralateral neck) | Predictors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last 2020, JAMA Oto-HNS (WashU) [#1] | 89 HPV+ BOT, transoral surgery + bilateral ND | 34/89 (38.2%) | **15/70 (21.4%)** | Primary crossing midline: OR 6.23 (95% CI 1.71–22.77); T3–T4. No regional recurrence among the 55 occult-negative patients, and only 2 of them received contralateral RT |
| Yao 2021, Head Neck (Mount Sinai group) [#2] | 120 cT1–2 HPV+ OPSCC, TORS + bilateral ND | 11/120 (tonsil 7.1%, BOT 10.9%) | None in cN0 patients | **cN category was the only independent predictor.** Well-lateralized cN0–1 tonsil: 1/44 (2%). Lateralized cN0–1 BOT without medial hemistructure involvement: 2 patients (4%) |
| Kornfeld 2024, J Laryngol Otol (St Vincent's Sydney) [#3] | 110 HPV+ OPSCC, TORS + bilateral ND, 2012–2022 | 14/110 (12.7%) | 2 (2%) | **Extranodal extension and multiple ipsilateral positive nodes.** BOT and tonsil rates were similar; level II most common (11 patients) |
| Wang 2025, OHNS (WashU) [#4] | 64 HPV+ OPSCC with elective contralateral ND, 2002–2018, median follow-up 58.5 mo | — | 12 (19%) | No contralateral regional recurrences. Most patients with pN0 contralateral necks were spared contralateral RT |
| Halle 2026, Head Neck [#5] | 106 pT1–2 pN0–1 HPV+ BOT, TORS + ipsilateral ND | — | **0/31** who had elective contralateral ND | 46 had no contralateral treatment and 29 had contralateral RT only; OS and DFS did not differ (groups were imbalanced: 78% vs 28% pN1) |
| Quadt 2026, Anticancer Res (Germany, 2000–2023) [#6] | 124 surgically treated OPSCC, bilateral ND (not HPV-restricted) | Midline-reaching or crossing: 47/55 | Lateralized: **1/69 (1.4%)** (a pT3 BOT) | Midline extension; contralateral metastasis linked to worse survival (p=0.046) |
| HPV+ tonsil, elective contralateral ND [#7] (PMID 39521177; details partly unverified) | HPV+ tonsil | 8.8% | Very low | Multiple ipsilateral nodes; depth of invasion |
| Lim group (Yonsei), Laryngoscope [#8] (pre-HPV-era tonsil) | 43 tonsil SCC with contralateral elective ND | — | **7/33 (21%) if ipsilateral N+; 0 if ipsilateral N0** | Ipsilateral nodal status |
| Chung 2011, Oral Oncol [#9] | Tonsil cancer; predictors of contralateral and RP nodes | Not retrieved | — | Citation only |

**Pooled data**
- **Occult contralateral meta-analysis (OPSCC with bilateral ND)** [#10]: pooled occult rate **1.7%** (95% CI 0.1–22.4) when the ipsilateral neck is cN0 (8 studies, 150 patients), and **9.8%** (95% CI 4.4–20.3; I² 72%) when it is cN+ (8 studies, 429 patients). Authors and journal were not seen (source: cancer.fr digest).
- **Punjabi 2024, Laryngoscope** [#11] (tonsil SCC): overall occult contralateral 10%; cT1–2 8%; **cT3–4 19%**; about 1% if the ipsilateral neck is N0. Occult nodes were at level II (81%) and level III (19%), with none at level I.
- **Zhang 2022 meta-analysis** [#12]: citation only.

**Registry data**
- **Kato 2018, Head Neck (NCDB, n=15,517)** [#13]: HPV positivity, BOT subsite and poor differentiation were associated with contralateral or bilateral nodes. Last et al. cite an NCDB figure of about 21.7% for HPV+ BOT; the attribution to Kato is unverified.
- **Tritter 2017, Laryngoscope** [#14]: contralateral or bilateral nodal rates did not differ by HPV status; T4 and BOT were associated.

**Predictor summary:** The most consistently reported predictors are **midline involvement or crossing** (Last OR 6.2; Quadt; Al-Mamgani pooled 12.1% vs 1.7%), **BOT subsite**, **clinical N category / ipsilateral nodal burden** (Yao; meta-analysis 1.7% vs 9.8%; Lim 21% vs 0%), **multiple ipsilateral nodes and ENE** (Kornfeld; Lynch for RT failures), and **T3–T4** (Punjabi 19%; Tritter; de Veij Mestdagh drainage 45% vs 14%). None of the retrieved surgical series reported **soft palate involvement** as a separate predictor with numbers. Ipsilateral **level IV** involvement as a contralateral predictor was not found in verified sources.

**Pooled data**
- **Razavian 2023, JAMA Netw Open** [#33]: 17 studies, 1,487 patients; pooled contralateral failure **2%**. **N2b pooled 3.1% (95% CI 1.2–4.9%).** Higher T category was associated with failure and nodal stage was not. Ipsilateral RT carried a higher contralateral failure risk than bilateral RT. A guideline summary cites T3–4 at 11.5% vs T1–2 at 1.8%.
- **Al-Mamgani 2017, Oral Oncol (critical review)** [#34]: 11 studies, 1,116 patients; mean contralateral failure **2.42%** (range 0–5.9%). **Midline involvement was the strongest prognosticator: 12.12% vs 1.71%.**

**N2b bottom line:** Pooled contralateral failure after ipsilateral RT is about 3%. However, single-centre estimates for N2b range from **0% (Natesan, Koo, Hara) to 7.9% (KROG) and 14.3% (Maskell)**, and Lynch identifies multiple nodes and ECE as risk factors. Selection bias in the N2b subsets is likely: N2b patients given unilateral RT are hand-picked low-volume or level II-only cases (Huang: ≤3 nodes). No study verified here reports surgical-pathology contralateral pN+ by ipsilateral node count in lateralized tumors.

### Theme 3 — BOT and soft palate extension: definitions, risk, reliability

- **Definitions found:**
  - Chronowski (MDACC): tonsillar fossa or anterior pillar with <1 cm soft palate involvement.
  - Al-Mamgani: soft palate ≥1 cm from midline.
  - The 2009 Radiat Oncol ipsilateral series: <1 cm medial extension to soft palate or BOT.
  - ASTRO 2017 (strong recommendation): no soft palate extension and no BOT involvement, T1–2, N0–1.
  - ASTRO 2017 (conditional recommendation): <1 cm soft palate, no BOT, N0–N2b without ECE.
  - Cooperative-group protocols (NRG-family; e.g., NCT03799445): unilateral treatment recommended for <1 cm extension into BOT or palate and N0–N2a; optional for N2b confined to level II; bilateral mandatory beyond these limits or with posterior wall involvement.
  - BC/HPV-era practice: any extension to soft palate or BOT, or within 1 cm of midline, means bilateral treatment.
- **Contralateral risk data:** Midline involvement is the best-supported risk factor (Al-Mamgani pooled 12.1% vs 1.7%; Quadt 47/55; Last OR 6.2). Yao: BOT without medial-hemistructure involvement gave about 4% contralateral pN+. No verified study isolates soft palate extension (<1 cm vs ≥1 cm) with surgical-pathology contralateral rates.
- **Interobserver reliability:** **No study measuring inter-rater agreement for soft palate or BOT extension, or for lateralization, in tonsil cancer was found.** Indirect evidence:
  - Clinical exam underestimates BOT spread.
  - MRI signs of BOT invasion (loss of the glossotonsillar air cleft) and soft palate invasion appear in a single RSNA abstract (unverified detail).
  - A 2017 commentary notes that surgeons and radiation oncologists may define "well-lateralized" differently.

  This is a genuine gap that the proposed study could address.

### Theme 4 — Surveys of practice

- **International practice-pattern survey, Oral Oncol 2021 (PMID 33524796; Toronto group, de Almeida and colleagues; first author not confirmed)** [#35]. There were 222 respondents (172 surgical oncologists, 44 radiation oncologists, 32 countries). Numbers from snippets:
  - Small N0 tonsil cancer: **unilateral treatment by 99% vs 97%** (no significant difference between specialties).
  - Single ipsilateral node <3 cm: **15% of radiation oncologists** treat bilaterally.
  - Radiation oncologists became more likely to treat bilaterally as nodal burden rose.
  - Tonsil with soft palate extension: 65% (surgeons) vs **100%** (radiation oncologists) treat bilaterally. With BOT extension: 77% vs **100%**.
  - Midline extension with a PET-negative contralateral neck: 53% (surgeons) vs **84%** (radiation oncologists) treat bilaterally.
- **McDowell & Corry 2021 letter** [#36]: argues that unilateral RT is underused, with intra- and inter-specialty variability.
- **Bailey 2026, Am J Clin Oncol (PMID 41712935)** [#37]: US H&N radiation oncologist survey (55 respondents, 41 institutions) covering dose and CTV variation. Its neck-laterality results were not retrieved.
- No ASTRO-membership-wide or Canadian national survey of contralateral elective nodal irradiation was found beyond the above.

**Implication for the premise:** The statement "most radiation oncologists electively treat the contralateral neck even in lateralized OPC" is **too broad**:
- For **small, node-negative or low-nodal-burden well-lateralized tonsil cancer**, almost all respondents, radiation oncologists included, treat **unilaterally**. Only 15% of radiation oncologists treat bilaterally with a single small node.
- The premise does hold for lateralized tumors with **soft palate or BOT extension, midline proximity, BOT primaries, or higher nodal burden (N2b-type)**. There, radiation oncologists treat bilaterally in 84–100% of cases, more often than surgeons do.

Suggested reframing: "In lateralized OPC with BOT or palate extension, or with multiple ipsilateral nodes, most radiation oncologists treat the contralateral neck, despite limited pathologic data."

### Theme 6 — Sentinel node and lymphatic mapping for the contralateral neck

- **CCTG HN.11 "SELECT"** [#44]. Details below were seen in CCTG, ASCO TPS, Rogel and site listings.
  - **Registration:** NCT05451004.
  - **Title:** "SPECT-CT Guided ELEctive Contralateral Neck Treatment (SELECT) for Patients with Lateralized Oropharyngeal Cancer: A Phase III Randomized Controlled Trial".
  - **Design:** international multicentre, **non-inferiority, randomized phase III**. Standard **bilateral neck RT** is compared with **SPECT-CT lymphatic-mapping-guided contralateral neck treatment**: the contralateral neck is treated only if mapping shows drainage to it.
  - **Primary endpoint:** disease-free survival (non-inferiority).
  - **Secondary endpoints:** isolated contralateral neck failure, swallowing-related QoL, OS, toxicity.
  - **Eligibility:** adults with untreated, pathologically proven **lateralized OPC (tonsil or BOT) not involving or crossing midline**, HPV status by p16 IHC, no clinical contralateral nodes, **planned for bilateral neck RT** (patients already planned for unilateral RT are excluded); patients ineligible for systemic therapy are allowed.
  - **Timeline:** start 2023-02-10; estimated primary completion 2030-08-30; study completion 2031-08-30; status recruiting.
  - **Leadership and funding:** CIHR-funded; J. de Almeida is a co-PI.
  - **Not retrieved:** target sample size and the exact registry primary-outcome text. Confirm on ClinicalTrials.gov.
  - **Correction to the premise:** HN.11 is a **SPECT-CT lymphatic-mapping-guided radiotherapy** trial. It is **not a surgical sentinel node biopsy trial.**
- **Toronto feasibility lead-in** (NCT05055206; Head Neck 2024; PMID 39016220) [#45]: Tc-99m sulfur colloid SPECT-CT in lateralized OPC; single-arm diagnostic study, target n of about 12, completed. Results were not retrieved.
- **Berania 2022, JOHNS (Toronto)** [#46]: 13 lateralized OPSCC with SPECT-CT then TORS + ipsilateral ND. 12 (92.3%) had ipsilateral-only drainage, 1 (7.7%) bilateral; no contralateral failures.
- **NKI-AVL (Netherlands) program:**
  - **SUSPECT** feasibility, de Veij Mestdagh 2018, Eur Arch ORL 275:2135–44 [#47]: 55 lateralized cT1–3 N0–2b HNSCC. Drainage visualized in 98%. **Contralateral drainage in 20% overall; 45% in T3 vs 14% in T1–2.** Contralateral sentinel nodes were mainly at level II (88%).
  - **SUSPECT trial**, Radiother Oncol 2020;147:56–63, NCT02572661 [#48]: 50 patients treated with SPECT/CT-guided ENI. Contralateral failure low (exact % not retrieved). Versus matched bilateral ENI: grade ≥2 dysphagia 54% vs 82%, tube feeding 10% vs 50%, less late xerostomia, better HRQOL.
  - **SUSPECT-2** (BMC Cancer 2019, NCT03968679) [#49]: adds a contralateral sentinel node procedure when contralateral drainage is seen. Primary endpoint is contralateral regional failure. Eligibility includes HPV+ OPC T1–4 N1.
  - **Contralateral failure in electively irradiated necks** (NKI 2019, Clin Transl Radiat Oncol) [#50]: 2.8% after bilateral ENI.
- **Other registered trials (seen as registry titles or snippets only):**
  - **LOOC** (UK; NCT04498221; BMJ Open 2025 protocol): phase II fhSPECT surgical imaging; primary outcome is the rate of contralateral drainage.
  - **SELECT-FR** (UNICANCER; NCT07241273): phase II, not yet recruiting.
  - **SEMIRAHN** (Leuven; NCT04688528): randomized phase II, dose and volume de-escalation with sentinel-node-mapping-driven contralateral RT in unilaterally node-positive HNSCC.
  - **PRIMO** (NCT05333523): SLNB-guided personalized ENI in larynx and pharynx cancer.
  - The Danish SLNB-in-OPC work was not found (see unverified leads).

### Theme 7 — Prospective and ongoing de-escalation trials and their neck rules

- **E3311 (ECOG-ACRIN)** [#51]: 519 enrolled, 495 had TOS; 2-year PFS by arm A/B/C/D was 96.9/94.9/96.0/90.7%. The 54-month long-term follow-up (JCO 2025) gave PFS 90.6% and OS 95.3%. **Its neck-dissection laterality rules and contralateral failure data were not retrieved.**
- **PATHOS** (NCT02215265; Owadally 2015 BMC Cancer protocol) [#52]: T1–3 N0–N2b (AJCC7). Risk-stratified PORT de-escalation; MDADI and OS endpoints. **Neck laterality rules were not retrieved.**
- **NRG-HN005** (NCT03952585) [#53]: p16+ low-risk definitive RT de-escalation, target accrual 711, closed. Neck-laterality section not retrieved.
- **NCT03799445** (ipilimumab/nivolumab + RT protocol) and **NCT02254278** (NRG-HN002) protocols: their PDFs on ClinicalTrials.gov contain the unilateral recommended / optional / bilateral mandatory scheme (<1 cm BOT or palate; N0–N2a recommended; N2b level II optional). The attribution between the two protocols was not fully confirmed.
- **NCT03729518** "TORS De-Intensification Protocol Version 2.0: Dose and Volume Reduction in the Neck": title seen only.
- **No ClinicalTrials.gov trial randomizing ipsilateral vs bilateral RT specifically for p16+ tonsil cancer was found, other than HN.11 and SELECT-FR.**

### Theme 8 — Contralateral failure after TORS + unilateral neck management

- **Sahovaler 2021, JOHNS (UHN Toronto; PMID 34949220)** [#54]: 32 lateralized tonsil and BOT patients, TORS + ipsilateral neck management (ND and/or RT). **3-year contralateral regional control 100%.**
- **Berania 2022** [#46]: SPECT-CT-guided, 13 patients, 0 contralateral failures.
- **Halle 2026** [#5]: 46 BOT patients with no contralateral treatment; no survival difference.
- **Wang 2025** [#4]: pN0 contralateral necks managed without contralateral RT; 0 contralateral regional recurrences.
- **Last 2020** [#1]: pN0 contralateral necks without RT (53/55); 0 regional recurrences.
- **Belau 2023, Eur Arch ORL (Hamburg; PMID 37133497)** [#55]: 84 patients, resection + bilateral ND + PORT. Omitting RT to the pN0 contralateral neck did not worsen tumor-free, cause-specific or overall survival.
- **Penn early TORS experience** (unverified details): among 31 node-positive patients with selective ND, 1 regional recurrence, in the untreated contralateral neck.

## 4. Gap analysis — what a Northwestern TORS cohort (about 75–80 bilateral NDs) can and cannot add

**Proposed cohort:** TORS + bilateral ND where the contralateral ND was done for (a) BOT involvement by a tonsil primary, (b) soft palate involvement, (c) BOT primary, or (d) an equivocal or positive contralateral node.

### What it can add (genuine gaps in the verified literature)
1. **Surgical-pathology contralateral pN+ by ipsilateral nodal count in lateralized tumors.** No verified study reports this.
   - RT series (Frankart, Natesan, Maskell, KROG, Razavian) report contralateral *failure* after unilateral RT, a surrogate that depends on follow-up and on selection.
   - Bilateral-ND series report predictors but not a stratified table: Kornfeld (multiple ipsilateral nodes, ENE) and Yao (cN category).
   - A table of contralateral pN+ by pathologic ipsilateral node count (0, 1, 2–4, ≥5; "N2b-equivalent" = AJCC7 multiple ipsilateral nodes ≤6 cm) and by ENE would be new, and directly relevant to the ASTRO 2017 conditional N2b recommendation.
2. **Contralateral pN+ by the specific extension criteria that drive bilateral treatment.** The usual cutoffs are soft palate <1 cm vs ≥1 cm, BOT involvement by tonsil primaries, and distance to midline. None has surgical-pathology data in the verified literature, yet they are the criteria under which 84–100% of surveyed radiation oncologists treat bilaterally. Measuring extension in millimetres on pathology (specimen) and on imaging would allow ROC or threshold analyses.
3. **Reliability of the lateralization criteria.** No verified interobserver study exists. Re-reading pretreatment imaging (and exam or operative notes where feasible) by two blinded readers, reporting κ for palate extension, BOT extension and the 1 cm-from-midline call, would be novel and cheap.
4. **Occult vs clinically suspected contralateral disease.** Separating group (d), equivocal or positive nodes, from groups (a)–(c), elective dissection, gives a true occult rate in the elective subgroup and the PPV of an "equivocal" contralateral node on CT, PET or US.
5. **Level distribution of contralateral metastases.** Level II predominance (Kornfeld; Punjabi; de Veij drainage 88% level II) could support level II(–III) selective contralateral ND or ENI volumes.
6. **Outcomes after a pN0 contralateral neck without contralateral RT,** if adjuvant RT fields were recorded. This replicates Last, Wang and Belau in an independent cohort.

### What it cannot add (or adds only weakly)
1. **Contralateral risk in truly well-lateralized tonsil cancer.** By design, well-lateralized tonsil tumors without palate or BOT extension rarely receive bilateral ND, so the cohort is **enriched for higher-risk tumors**. Rates cannot be extrapolated to the population addressed by ASTRO's strong recommendation (T1–2 N0–1, no palate or BOT involvement). This is indication bias. **Report rates by indication and avoid a single pooled "contralateral rate".**
2. **Precise subgroup estimates.** With about 78 patients:
   - At an overall rate of about 15% (about 12 events), the 95% CI is roughly 8–25%.
   - A subgroup of about 25 N2b-equivalent lateralized patients with 2–4 events has a CI of about 2–30%.
   - Multivariable modelling is limited to about 1 predictor per 10 events, so a firth-penalized or univariable analysis is realistic.
3. **Comparison with unilateral RT failure rates.** Pathologic pN+ (a prevalence) is not equivalent to contralateral failure after unilateral RT (an incidence). Many microscopic deposits might be sterilized by chemotherapy or never progress. The pathologic rate is an **upper bound** on failure risk if untreated.
4. **Survival or toxicity effect of bilateral ND vs bilateral RT vs observation.** There is no randomized comparator, and HN.11 (DFS endpoint, readout about 2030) is the definitive test.
5. **HPV-era generalizability.** Single-institution TORS selection (PET use, surgeon thresholds) limits external validity. A comparator arm of TORS patients with ipsilateral-only ND, tracking contralateral failure, would strengthen the study.

### Suggested design additions
- Record pathologic ipsilateral node count, size, levels and ENE; AJCC7 and AJCC8 N; primary subsite; mm extension into palate and BOT (pathology and imaging); distance to midline; T stage; smoking.
- Add a reference cohort of TORS + ipsilateral-only ND patients for contralateral failure, a time-to-event endpoint with competing risks.
- Run an interobserver sub-study of imaging laterality calls (κ).
- Report exact binomial CIs; stratify by indication (a)–(d).
